Peekless

See who's peeking at you. Peekless gives every app on your phone a privacy score from 0 to 100, based on the permissions it asks for and the trackers inside it, explained in plain English. Offline, no account, no ads, no tracking.

Android · 25 languages · Works offline

Why Peekless

One score for your phone

A 0 to 100 score for the whole phone and one for each app. 70 and above is Safe, 40 to 69 is Medium, and below 40 is Risky.

Plain English, not jargon

“Location all the time” and “Read SMS” instead of a list of permission codes, so you can decide for yourself.

Finds the trackers

Spots about 60 known tracking and advertising SDKs inside your apps. An optional Deep scan also reads class names in the code, to find trackers the manifest does not show.

Private and open

It runs on your phone and sends nothing about your apps anywhere. GPL-3.0, so you can read every line.

Features

Scores you can understand

  • A phone score from 0 to 100, and a score for every app, sorted with the riskiest first
  • A score breakdown for each app shows exactly which permissions and trackers cost it points
  • Unusual permissions cost more: a flashlight that asks for your contacts is a bigger problem than a messenger that does
  • Filters by risk, install source and more, and a trusted apps list for the ones you have already checked

Permissions and trackers

  • A permissions view shows who can use your camera, microphone, location, contacts, SMS and more
  • Tracker detection reads each app's manifest for about 60 known tracking and ad SDKs
  • Deep scan (optional): turn it on in Settings to also read the class names inside each app's code. It works offline, skips apps that came with your phone, and is slower only the first time
  • A Trackers tab shows which tracker sits in which apps, most common first
  • The tracker list can be updated when you tap the button, and a downloaded list can only add trackers, never remove them

Network monitor (optional)

  • Off until you switch it on. Android then asks you to allow a VPN connection
  • It shows which domain names each app asks for and marks the ones that belong to known ad and analytics services
  • It is a local VPN that takes only DNS lookups, so your internet speed does not change. It sees names, never pages, messages or any content, and nothing leaves your phone
  • Counts are kept on your phone and you can clear them at any time. Switch it off in Peekless or in Android settings
  • Honest limits: it can miss apps that use their own encrypted DNS (DNS over HTTPS), it does not work when Private DNS is set to a fixed provider, and showing which app asked needs Android 10 or newer

Security checks

  • Apps with accessibility access, device admin or notification access
  • Apps installed from outside a store, and an install source shown on every app
  • Debuggable builds and apps built for very old Android versions
  • An optional unused apps check if you allow Usage access, and a fix wizard that walks you through the worst problems

Keep an eye on it

  • A score over time chart, so you see whether your phone is getting safer
  • An optional weekly summary and optional app alerts when a new app arrives or an app gains a sensitive permission (checked on your phone about every 6 hours)
  • A changes list shows what is new since last time
  • A home screen widget and a Quick Settings tile

Share and everyday use

  • Share a report, or export it as a PDF or JSON file
  • 25 languages, following your phone language or the one you pick in Settings
  • New look: a dark green design with a gauge for your score, a privacy overview (what share of your apps can use camera, microphone, location and trackers) and one big Scan button. Light and dark themes
  • In-app updates: check, download and install a new version without leaving the app

What a score means, and what it does not

  • It shows what an app can do, not what it does. A high-risk score means the app has the power to do something, not that it is doing it.
  • Trackers are found from the app's manifest. Some will be missed, and a missing tracker is not proof there is none.
  • A score is a guide, not a verdict. Use it to ask the right questions about your apps.

Private by design

Peekless collects nothing. No account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Scoring happens on your phone, and the list of your apps never leaves it. The optional Network monitor uses a local VPN for DNS lookups only; nothing leaves your phone. The internet is used only when you tap Check for updates, download an update or update the tracker list, and it contacts GitHub only. Read the full privacy policy.

Install

  1. Open the latest release on your phone.
  2. Download peekless-x.y.z.apk and open it. Android may ask once to allow installs from your browser.
  3. Open Peekless and tap the refresh button to score your apps.

Updates: after that, update from inside Peekless: Settings › Check for updates.

F-Droid: Peekless is also prepared for F-Droid. F-Droid signs apps with its own key, so an F-Droid install and a GitHub install cannot update each other. Pick one source.
